TYPES OF WINNING DIGITAL PRODUCTS
#1: Infoproducts
This is a digital product such as a course/ebook that’s intended to bring the
user one step closer to their goal. It is packaged information.
Pros:
- Niche-specific: If you build an audience in a niche where a high % of
them have a similar problem, conversion rate will be high
- Scaleable: It can be sold to an unlimited amount of people
- Passive: After the initial setup, you don’t have to worry about
anything other than marketing
Cons:
- Competitive market: It’s pretty tough to make a unique infoproduct
that hasn’t already been made & sold 100x. Therefore most of the
work is done on the marketing side of things. (The product won’t sell
itself)
#2: Communities
This is an exclusive community for paying members. These usually include
exclusive content, private events, networking opportunities, etc.
Pros:
- Often recurring: This results in ongoing revenue
- Community: You will build a community of your most loyal &
dedicated audience members

Cons:
-

Management: Communities require A LOT of work to maintain good
quality & high customer satisfaction
High churn: Paid communities usually have pretty high churn
because users get in, get what they want & cancel their subscription

#3: Software
Software is a functional tool that the customer pays to use. Common
examples: SaaS, apps, web apps, scripts, API’s, plugins & extensions.
Pros:
- Recurring revenue: Resulting in ongoing revenue
- Versatile: There are a ton of niches and problems that can be solved
with software
- Scaleable: Once built, software can be used by many users
- Low churn: If your software is useful the churnrate will usually be
pretty low
Cons:
- High development cost: If you don’t partner with a developer,
development costs may be high.
- Time-intensive: You will need to give technical support, update the
software & fix bugs so it is not necessarily passive
#4: Digital services
Providing a digital service is solving your clients’ problem through your (or
the persons you outsource to) expertise in a digital skill. Common
examples: Graphic design, video editing, content creation, web design,
marketing, coding, consulting & coaching

Pros:
- Low barrier to entry: Anyone with a digital skill can offer digital
services, you don’t need to have any capital or social proof built up
- Easy to sell: It’s a lot easier for someone to drop money on a
guaranteed service, than information.
- Agency: Once you get good at signing clients, you can outsource the
workload and transition into an agency model
Cons:
- Time-intensive: The revenue is tied to your time and effort, so
outsourcing is necessary to scale.
- Dependent on client: Your income will rely on whether your client(s)
need work done.
#5: Mentorships
This could be listed under digital services, but I want to add it as it’s own
thing as it’s one of the biggest money makers in the digital product industry.
This is a one-on-one program where you (the mentor) can guide the
mentee to their desired outcome.
Pros:
- High-ticket: One-on-one mentorships are usually priced pretty high
- Low overhead: Doesn’t require much resources/capital to start.
Cons:
- Expertise: You need to be an expert in your field in order to provide
mentorship. Most aren’t.
- Time-consuming: You’re directly trading time for money.
- Requires results: In order to do this ethically, you need to provide
results for the client.

PRICING YOUR PRODUCT
Once you pick a product to sell, you’ve got a decision to make. How much
will it cost?
There is a simple rule that I follow… & that is that I never underprice my
products. If I price my infoproduct at $10, people will view it as that… A $10
product. It’s pretty much worthless in their eyes.
Whereas if I price it at $500, it appears more premium & therefore it has a
higher perceived value.
Of course you cannot charge extortionary prices, but if you follow the rule
of always making premium products & never underpricing, you will print.

CREATING A FREE LEAD MAGNET
A lead magnet is a digital resource (PDF, video, template, etc) that you
offer to your audience in exchange for their information, usually an email.
This attracts potential customers as everyone that is interested in your lead
magnet, will obviously be interested in your product.
Here’s how you do this…
First you need to identify your ideal customer profile & common pain points
that they face in their daily life. Understand what type of person will buy
your products & what problems/goals they have.
For example, if your ideal customer profile is a beginner dropshipper, he
will probably have the problem of not knowing how to advertise his
products via. TikTok ads. You could create a free “Dropshipping TikTok ads
masterclass" as your lead magnet.
Your lead magnet could be any digital asset: an ebook, a checklist, a
template, a set of videos, a case study, etc.

As long as the product is aligned with the product you sell, meaning that
the type of person that will download your lead magnet will also be
interested in your paid product, then it is a viable lead magnet.
The lead magnet also has to be good. It will be a first impression of you &
your work so make sure it’s specific & full of actionable value. Make sure
your leads think, “If the free stuff is so good, the paid stuff must be
incredible.”
SETTING UP A NEWSLETTER & AUTOMATIONS
You may be thinking, “An email list in 2024? People still read emails?”
Surprisingly for most young people nowadays, email lists are still the best
way to nurture & sell to your leads by a longshot.
I mean, think about this. When you build up an audience on a social media
account, you’re still relying on an algorithm to deliver your content to them.
There is a middleman between you and your audience. Owning an email
list of your leads completely removes the middleman. Every email that you
send out will be in their inbox & it’s up to them whether they open it, there is
no middleman involved.
Additionally, your social media account is always at a risk of getting
banned. Imagine you build up 100k followers on Instagram & it’s where you
get 90% of your sales from. Suddenly, you get an email in your inbox…
“ACCOUNT SUSPENDED”. Congratulations you just lost 90% of your
income.
An email list fixes this by storing all of your leads in a platform that you
actually own. Nobody will ever take your emails from you.
Another reason having an email list is great is that you don’t waste traffic.
Let’s say 3% of the people who watch your youtube videos end up
subscribing, but 10% convert into lead magnet downloads. That is 7% of

your videos traffic that would have otherwise watched your video & went on
with their lives, potentially never seeing your content ever again.

HOW TO WRITE EMAILS THAT SELL
To write emails that sell, you’ve got to first understand how a sales funnel
works.
A sales funnel is a series of steps that guide potential customers from the
point of discovering your brand, to the point of purchasing your products.
Each stage serves a purpose.
For example, someone sees your content for the first time on tiktok. Then
the lead magnet you pushed in the video peaks their interest so they join
your newsletter. Overtime they receive so much value & are nurtured into
believing that buying the product will benefit their life, so they do so.
The four steps of a funnel:
Awareness:
This is the part of the funnel where potential customers first discover your
brand. For example seeing your youtube video for the first time, then
reading the welcome email and lead magnet.
This is done simply by posting content & sending people to the lead
magnet.

Interest:
Potential customers think about how the product you’re pitching will impact
their lives.
Provide value-based content that further builds their belief in you as an
authority figure. Keep clarifying the benefits of your product in your content.
Decision:
The part of the funnel where customers are close to making the purchase,
but they need a slight push to make it.
Do this by addressing objections. This can be done by showing customer
feedback/testimonials/customer success stories.
Action:
In this stage, you need to encourage potential customers to take action.
This is done by providing a clear call to action. A “BUY NOW” button
alongside a reason to act immediately. For example a limited time discount.
Rules for selling through email:
- Understand your audience’s pain points & speak directly about them,
make them a major focal point of your message
- End each promotional email with a clear action-oriented CTA
- Sell without being pushy by balancing value & promotion. For
example 3 value emails for every 1 promotional email
- Pitch subtly. If mentioning a problem in the email, pitch your product
as the solution
- Build trust & credibility prior to pitching

YOUTUBE LONGFORM:
The YT longform algorithm:
Here are the metrics that YouTube looks at to decide whether or not they
should push your video:
- Click-Through-Rate (CTR): Reflects how often viewers click a video
after it pops up in their feed as a percentage.
- Average View Duration (AVD): Measures how long viewers watch a
video on average.
- Retention Rate: Shows what percentage of a video was watched on
average.

Keys to going viral:
To understand how to go viral, you need to understand how YouTube
determines whether to push your video or not…
If you don’t have any subscribers and YouTube has no data about you as a
creator to work with, it will use the video’s metadata (title, description,
keywords, and transcribed video) and matches it with potential viewers who
may be interested in the topic.
The algorithm tests the video with small audience profiles to determine
whether the video is good. It does this by measuring the metrics that i listed
above. If these metrics are good with the initial push, YouTube gradually
expands the video’s reach to a broader audience with related interests.
If you already do have an audience (subscribers/viewers) youtube will
prioritize pushing your videos to that core audience, it uses the metrics to
determine whether to increase reach further or not. So content that aligns
with your core audience’s interests will be promoted more

YOUTUBE SHORTS:
The YT shorts algorithm:
- Swipe ratio: The percentage of viewers who swipe away from a
video without watching it fully. A high swipe-away rate indicates low
viewer interest. Which is a negative signal to the YT algo. Try to keep
the swipe-away rate under 20%.
- Average viewer duration (AVD): The average percentage of the
video that viewers watch. For videos under 30 seconds, aim for 100%
AVD. For videos 30-60 seconds long, try maintain a AVD of around
80%
- Replays & engagement: This isn’t a trackable metric but viewers
rewatching or looping the video (either intentionally or by engaging in
the comments while the video plays in the background) will always be
a positive signal to YouTube. Try comment bait, through controversy,
mispronunciations or misspelling.

TIKTOK
The TikTok algorithm:

- Engagement: Tiktok looks at how often users like, comment, share &

typically between 200-1000 viewers. This is used to gauge the initial
engagement & performance metrics. If the video performs well with
